## Abstract

This project presents the development of interactive circuit simulators for power electronic converters implemented in Python. The objective is to provide an accessible computational tool that supports the learning process in undergraduate electronics courses.

The developed simulators reproduce the behavior of several switched converters, including controlled rectifiers and conventional DC/DC converters such as the Boost converter. Users can modify circuit parameters such as switching frequency and component values, allowing them to observe the resulting changes in system dynamics through graphical outputs.

The simulators were developed by students as part of an Analog Electronics course. A second group of students used the tools in subsequent course iterations. Perception interviews revealed that interacting with the simulators improved students’ conceptual understanding of converter operation and digital simulation processes.

Results show that students who developed the simulators gained deeper insights into circuit behavior, while students who used them reported improved comprehension and increased engagement with the subject.
